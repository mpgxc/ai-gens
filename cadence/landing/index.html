What's inside

Small app. No compromises.

Everything a focus timer needs, and deliberately nothing else.

Deadline-accurate

The timer stores an end time, not a tick count. Close the lid, come back, and it is exactly where it should be — no drift, no lost minutes.

Name your sessions

Label what you are working on before you start. Every finished block is logged with its name, so the history reads like a diary instead of a tally.

Recurring breaks

Short breaks between focus blocks, a long one after every fourth. Every duration and the cycle length are yours to tune.

Stats that stack up

Totals by day, week, month and year, charted natively. Proof of the weeks you actually showed up.

Native notifications

A gentle chime and a system notification when a phase ends. Start the break, skip it, or add five minutes without leaving the alert.

Liquid Glass, properly

Built on the macOS 26 design system — real translucency, real depth, and morphing controls that respect Reduce Motion and Reduce Transparency.

Speaks your language

English and Português, switched automatically with your system language. No settings screen required.

Yours, entirely

No account, no sync service, no analytics. Your sessions are a plain file on your Mac and they never leave it.

The cycle

Focus. Break.
Repeat.

Four focus blocks, three short breaks, one long one at the end. That is a cycle — two hours and ten minutes of structured work. Cadence keeps count so you don't have to.

Requires macOS 26 Tahoe or later

Cadence isn't signed by a registered developer, so macOS quarantines it on download. Drag it to Applications, then run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Cadence.app once before the first launch. That clears a single flag — it doesn't turn off any system security setting. Prefer building it yourself? xcodegen generate in cadence/macos, then ⌘R in Xcode.
